Ethereum Game Box

A game console you play by tapping a badge. Every match is staked and settled on Ethereum, and nobody at the table needs ETH, a wallet app or a single pop-up.

01What it is

The problem, the idea, and what a player actually does.

The problem

On-chain games ask too much before the fun starts: install a wallet, buy gas, approve a token, sign a prompt you can't read. That works for one person at a desk. It doesn't work for four friends around a table, a booth at an event, or a bar on a Friday night.

The idea

The Game Box is a small console: a Raspberry Pi with a screen and a speaker, tap stations for the players, and a hardware key inside that gives the Box its own identity on chain. Players carry NFC badges, each one a wallet whose key never leaves the chip. Tap to join, tap to stake, tap to play.

One sentence: the badge is the wallet, the Box is the referee, and Ethereum is the bank.

What a player does

  1. Walks up and taps. The Box reads the badge's wallet and sends it test tokens. No ETH needed, ever: the relayer pays the gas.
  2. Gets a name. One more tap claims name.gamebox.eth, which follows the player from Box to Box.
  3. Picks a game and stakes. A single tap commits each player, and the Box moves both stakes into the pot. The screen shows who is in and what is at stake.
  4. Plays. Taps are the controls: first to tap, pick a card, lock in a choice.
  5. Gets paid. The Box signs the result, the chain checks it and pays the winner, all in about ten seconds. Wins move titles like champion.stockduel.gamebox.eth.

Who it's for

Players

Real stakes and real ownership, with nothing to install and nothing to learn beyond "tap here".

Operators

Anyone can run a Box at a café, an event or at home, stake to it, and earn a share of every game played on it.

Game makers

One game interface: start, step, outcome, plus a game.json. Write the rules, and the Box handles badges, stakes and payouts. Or describe a game to the Box, and the game builder writes it.

What we're building this weekend

  • One working Box on the table, with two tap stations and a handful of badges.
  • Real games with real stakes on Sepolia, settled in seconds.
  • A token launched on Uniswap, whose fees feed back into the games.
  • Names for every Box and every player on ENS, and a public site that reads them.

02How it works

The pieces on the table, the pieces on chain, and the path one match takes between them. This is the design; Where we are says what runs today.

On the table

The Box

A Raspberry Pi 5 with a touchscreen and a Bluetooth speaker. It runs the games, the screen and the sound, and talks to the chain.

The Box key

A HYP2003 USB token. Its P-256 key is made on the chip and never leaves it. Every stake, seat and result the Box sends is signed by it.

Tap stations

One per player: an ESP32-S3 with a PN532 NFC reader, a small OLED, an LED and a buzz motor. It talks over ESP-NOW to a receiver on the Pi's USB.

Badges

Arx HaLo NFC chips. Each holds a wallet key (key 1) that can never be copied out, and an identity key (key 2) that signs random rolls. Today the Box signs stakes and seats, so key 1 signs nothing.

In the cloud and on chain

Relayer

A Cloudflare Worker that pays the gas. It sends what a Box key or a badge signed, plus a few rate-limited requests such as test-USDC top-ups. It sends one transaction at a time, and refuses anything else before spending a wei.

Contracts on Sepolia

The names (which also hold the Box registry), the games, the token and its Uniswap pool, and staking. All on one chain, so everything can call everything.

ENS

gamebox.eth names every Box and every player, and carries live titles like the current champion.

Public site

Reads ENS and the contracts to show Boxes, players and champions, and sends writes through the relayer. No backend of its own.

Why the Box key matters

Ethereum can now check P-256 signatures natively (the P256VERIFY precompile, about 7,000 gas). So the Box doesn't need a seed phrase or a hot wallet: a cheap hardware token, the same kind used for digital signatures in India, becomes an on-chain identity. Contracts know which Box sent a result, and the licensor can rotate or suspend that key at any time.

One match, end to end

  • Badgetaps to stake
  • Tap stationcarries the tap
  • Boxchecks it, signs with its key
  • Relayerchecks the Box, pays gas
  • Sepoliamoves stakes, pays out
  1. Entry. Each player taps to stake. The badge signs nothing: the tap is the consent, and the Box vouches for it.
  2. Open. The Box signs one Stake for both players with its key. The relayer checks the request, then sends it, and DuelPot pulls both stakes.
  3. Play. Taps drive the game. Where a game needs chance, both badges add their own signed randomness, so neither the Box nor a player picks the outcome.
  4. Settle. The Box signs the result. The contract checks the signature, the round and the expiry, then pays the winner in the token they staked. One result pays the pot, moves the title and scores the championship.
  5. Safety net. If the Box never settles, anyone can send both stakes back after an hour.

What you have to trust

Every game declares one of two tiers, and the Box shows it before anyone stakes.

Verified

The chain can check the result itself. Pots can be large, and a challenge window lets anyone prove a Box cheated and take its stake.

Refereed

Only the Box saw what happened, like who tapped first. Pots are capped, and the Box's stake backs its word.

03Games and the token

Two games play today and two more are planned, and the token ties the games, the Boxes and Uniswap together. Where we are says what runs today.

The games

GameHow it playsTier
Quick DrawThe screen says "draw". First tap wins. The simplest game, and the first one we build end to end.Refereed
Stock DuelBest of three. Each player plays a stock from a spinning Rolodex; once both cards are down, the two badges' randomness picks the stat (price, average price, 24-hour move or liquidity), read live from Robinhood Chain's stock pools. Cards flip together; a timer plays for a slow player.Refereed, with a public replay proof
Bet on XTwo players state a claim the chain can check ("gas is under 2 gwei at block N"), offer odds, and both tap. A checker contract settles it from chain state. Not built: sealed in the Box's rack.Verified
B17A heist on a vault. A bot keeps sending transactions; players buy ranges of its future nonce. Wider ranges cost more (width squared), and the narrowest range holding the live nonce takes the vault. Not built: sealed in the Box's rack.Verified

Stock Duel is the main game. Its replay proof is the Robinhood block number its numbers were read at, so anyone can read the same pools at the same block and recheck the result. A Box without a Robinhood RPC falls back to a frozen deck and claims no proof.

The game builder

Anyone can make a new game on the Box, which wasn't in the first plan. A Designer asks one question at a time, by keys, typing or voice. A Forge agent (Claude Sonnet) then writes the game from a Quick Draw or Stock Duel template, confined to one folder, and checks and installs it. Its rules run in a locked-down child process with no network or disk writes, and its screen in a sandboxed frame that only draws. Built games are always Refereed, and play for nothing: they aren't on chain. The first, Rooftop Dash, took 57 seconds on the Pi.

The token

Games are staked in test USDC, the only USDC we use. The Game Box token, GBX, is what stands behind a Box: one billion minted once, burnable, with no way to mint more. It launches through Uniswap's Continuous Clearing Auction, which sets a fair opening price and then seeds a Uniswap v4 pool. That pool carries our own hook, with three fee levels:

1%

Any normal swap.

0.25%

A swap made with a badge tap at a Box, up to a daily cap tied to that Box's stake. Playing in person earns the discount.

0%

Swaps through a router we approve for the game contract, so the buyback costs nothing.

The hook also keeps a time-weighted price, which v4 doesn't provide. The buyback and the operator auction use it, so neither can be pushed around by one big trade.

Where the fees go

Every settled game pays a small fee, split in half:

  • Half buys the token back and burns it, through the pool at zero fee. More games, less supply.
  • Half goes to the Box, shared between its operator and the people who staked to it.

Boxes, operators and stakers

  • Operators win the right to run a Box through an operator auction, priced from the pool's time-weighted price. The tokens they bid become the Box's stake.
  • Stakers back a Box they trust and earn from its games. The operator sets a commission between 10% and 90%. A raise only takes effect after a delay; a cut applies at once.
  • Cheating costs stake. Unstaking waits out a delay, and until it ends, a proven cheat can take the Box's stake.
  • The lobby lets a player swap USDC for the token with one tap, at the tap fee, and stake to the Box in the same tap.

04Partner tracks

Where each partner's technology carries real weight in the product, and what a judge will see. Where we are says what's built.

Uniswap

  • Launch: the token launches through the Continuous Clearing Auction into a v4 pool.
  • Hook: our v4 hook sets the fee per swap: 1%, 0.25% with a badge tap and the Box's signature, 0% for the game contract. It also keeps the time-weighted price.
  • Buyback and burn: every settled game swaps half its fee into the token and burns it.
  • Operator auction: a second auction sells the right to run a Box, priced from the hook's time-weighted price.
  • Judges see: a tap-discount swap at the Box, then a game settling and the burn landing on chain. We keep FEEDBACK.md as we go, as the track asks.

ENS

  • Box names like mumbai-01.gamebox.eth carry the Box's key and status, and expire with its licence.
  • Player names are claimed with a tap and never expire. The badge owns its records, and the game may only write its stats.
  • Live titles: champion.stockduel.gamebox.eth moves to each new winner, only after a signed win. A title lapses after its term: 7 days for Stock Duel, 15 minutes for Quick Draw.
  • The site reads names through ENS and lists them from contract events: no database behind it.
  • Pop-ups: anyone can open a 10-minute Box name, which stands in for a licence until the operator auction.
  • Championships: a cup like mumbai-cup.stockduel.gamebox.eth keeps live standings. Its winner keeps the name and the prize, and the records freeze.
  • Judges see: they claim a name with their own tap, win a round, and watch the title move to them on the site.

0G

  • Game verifier: an agent on 0G's private inference reads a store game's log and says who gets paid how much. See the game store plan.
  • Voice: players speak a bet, and 0G turns it into terms the chain can check.
  • Practice opponent: an AI player to learn against.
  • Decision log: every AI decision is stored on 0G Storage with its request ID, so it can be audited.
  • Safe stop: if 0G doesn't answer, the Box refuses. It never guesses.
  • We add this once our 0G credits arrive; the rest of the plan doesn't depend on it.

Decisions to make

  • How does a game pay its fee? The design takes a fee from every game, half to the burn and half to the Box. DuelPot pays the winner twice the stake and takes nothing, so nothing reaches notifyFees or a buyback. Milestone 4 can't finish until it does.
  • How does the pool launch? The design says the auction, which isn't built. Suggested: if time runs short, seed the pool directly. Every fee level still shows on a real swap, and only the auction drops out of the Uniswap story.
  • Can a badge sign a stake? Today the Box signs for both players, so a Box key alone can move any badge's test USDC. That's fine for test money and not for real money. FakeUSDC's notes say a badge signs only sign_random, but that is key 2's limit: the Box's notes say key 1 signs anything. Nobody has tried it on our badges, and one test on the Pi settles it.

What comes next

  1. Record the staked match. Two new badges claim names, top up, stake and play to the end with hand taps. That closes milestone 2 and moves a title for milestone 5.
  2. Close milestone 1. Have the real Box send a heartbeat, and show a forged one refused.
  3. Play Stock Duel live on the Pi. Set ROBINHOOD_RPC_URL there, play a staked match with live prices, and recheck its block for milestone 3.
  4. Finish the token. Settle the fee and the launch, initialize the pool, add the buyback and the lobby's swap, and fill in FEEDBACK.md, which has no entries yet.
  5. Bring in the operator site. Move names-operator onto the live addresses and open a PR.
  6. Then the network. Milestone 6 is the first thing to cut.

06How we get there

The order we build in, how we work, and what the demo and submission look like.

How we work

  • One feature at a time, finished. Contracts, the Box, the screen and the tests, then a run on the real Box with real badges. Only then does the next one start.
  • The demo always works. Each milestone leaves something we could show. If time runs out, we cut from the end of the list, never from the middle.
  • A test page on the Box gets a button for every new chain call, tap and signature, so each piece is checked on the hardware before it goes into a game.
  • Split where it's safe. Once the first game works, the token, the names and staking touch separate contracts and can be built side by side.

The demo

About four minutes, at the table:

  1. A judge taps a badge, gets tokens and claims a name.
  2. They swap at the tap discount in the lobby and stake into a Stock Duel.
  3. They play best of three against one of us, with live stock prices.
  4. The winner is paid in seconds, the fee is burned on chain, and the champion title moves on the site.

As a fallback, the site's Story shows the ENS half in a browser, with a test badge and the demo Box.

Before judging: the token, pool and hook are live, the badges are charged, the Box is on stable Wi-Fi, and the relayer is funded.
