The Game Box UI, with a Demo mode

The plan for the Box's touchscreen, built simulated first. Plan live at https://plans.georgekm.com/ethereum-game-box-game-ui-2026-11-06/. Demo live at https://ethereum-game-box.pages.dev/.

01Goal

The Box's touchscreen UI, plus a Demo mode of the same UI that runs in any browser with no hardware. The hackathon requires a live deployment, and Demo mode is it: anyone with the link can tap a badge, stake and play a match against a bot or a friend, and watch the simulated chain settle it.

This pass is UI only and fully simulated: no contracts, no relayer, no Box server, no chain reads, no hardware drivers. The screens talk to one interface, BoxLink, so the real Box plugs in behind it later without a screen changing.

02What exists today

The repo is greenfield. It has the pnpm workspace (apps/*, packages/*), tsconfig.base.json, the root scripts that run typecheck, test and build across packages, CLAUDE.md, a README per folder, and the project plan. No package has a package.json yet, so pnpm typecheck runs nothing. deployments/sepolia.json has no contracts. There is no screen, no game code and no test.

This pass adds @egb/box with its screen (apps/box/src/screen/) and two games' rules and screens (apps/box/src/games/), a simulated Box behind BoxLink, the Demo mode build, its tests, and a public deployment on Cloudflare Pages. Nothing outside apps/box/ changes except this plan and the lockfile.

The design language it continues, as plain facts:

  • The Box is a small piece of the world computer drawn as a world of light: a core at the centre of a dark grid, with the chain's own objects (ports, a block readout, programs) wired into it by lines of light. Inside a game the Box steps back and the game has its own look.
  • One locked layout for Home: ports in the top corners, the block readout at the top centre, the core in the middle, the focused game's title, facts and Play on the left, and a rack of programs along the bottom on the Grid's floor, every trace running into the core.
  • The look fails in known ways, and these are rules: lit fills look like a website (all brightness comes from thin lines); wide or animated glows look cheap and cost frames (glow is static); a spinning logo and a boot that moves everything at once look broken; idle programs that glow look busy; cyan for the system would be mistaken for Player 2.
  • Text is big and set in wide display caps, like a console, with squared caps for labels and mono for every number.
  • Stock Duel keeps cards. The hand is a Rolodex with index tabs, so a player always sees their whole hand. The stat is picked last, after both cards are down; picking it first hands the round to the second player. No dice, no hex-hash visuals, no cards fanned on a table, no tilted cards.

03The look: the Grid

A near-black world lit only by lines. All brightness comes from thin emissive lines; surfaces never light up as flat fills. Cool white-blue light belongs to the system, orange and cyan to the two sides. Geometry is rectilinear: straight runs, 45° bends and chamfered corners, with circles only for the core and the badge sockets. A reflective floor grid runs toward the viewer under a single horizon line. No skeuomorphic material is left: no plates, screws, chips, copper, brass, gunmetal, chains, needles, LCD segments, keycaps, wood or pucks.

Object How it looks When it moves
Grid The void; a faint 64 px grid on the back wall; one horizon line; a perspective floor vanishing under the core, with the core's soft reflection on it. Every trace from the ports, the block readout and the programs runs into the core as a light line, unpowered at 18 %, ending in a lit terminal bar where it meets a part. Never. Drawn once per layout and scale and cached. Packets run along its traces as light trails.
Core An outer ring of light with four notches, a ring of 24 segments, a thin inner ring round a dark disc, and the real six-facet Ethereum mark in wireframe: near-black facets edged in hot lines. Holds still. Ignites segment by segment at boot. Swells (a ring grows out and fades, the segments bloom) when a transaction lands or a block seals. Turns once like a coin when a game launches.
Ports Top corners. A socket of light in the seat's colour (Player 1 orange, left; Player 2 cyan, right) holding the badge's blockie as a lit LED grid, the name, a mono balance and a seat-colour underline. Empty: a dim socket and TAP BADGE. A badge arriving rezzes the socket and name; changed balance digits flicker in. A tap flashes the socket; a refused tap shakes it with a red flash.
Block readout and gas Four corner brackets round BLOCK and the head block in mono, SEPOLIA under it. Beside it a 16-segment gas bar with a 1.8 GWEI readout. Changed block digits flicker in one at a time. Gas segments switch one at a time toward the new value.
Facts Small chamfered data tags: a line icon and a word (players, time, stake range). Rez in 90 ms apart when the focused game changes.
Keys Chamfered frames of light. primary (Play and every key that moves the game on): the line full with its bloom and a glowing label. ghost (Back, Home): a quiet line. alert (REFUND ALL): primary in red. The chosen stake fills solid with light. A press flashes the key's shape. TAP TO START breathes.
Programs The rack's cartridges: chamfered frames round an art window with a faint grid and the game's art in light lines (Quick Draw a bolt, Stock Duel orange and cyan candlesticks), the title under it. Unbuilt games are sealed: the window hatched, a small SOON tag. The focused one lifts on a spring and only then lights its frame and a hot foot bar.
Transaction progress A packet runs along the trace from the port to the core (or out to a port for a payout), leaving a trail that fades; the core swells; a strip steps SIGNED › SENT › IN BLOCK n › PAID, with the hash, or SIMULATED, beside it. Each step lights (and flickers once) when it happens, at the simulated chain's real pace.

Palette. Void #010409, panel fills #030a12, deep #061522. System light #cfe8f6 with a white #ffffff core and a rgb(150 200 230 / 0.42) bloom. Seats: Player 1 orange #ff9b3d, Player 2 cyan #3fd9ff, each with its own bloom. Alert red #ff2d3d. Text #eaf7ff, #a9c4d2, #6d8796, #3e5462. System light is never cyan: cyan is Player 2's alone and orange Player 1's. A win is the winner's seat colour, not green; a loss dims to 40 %, not red. Red means something is wrong (Refund, CUT POWER, NEEDS n EGB, a refused tap). Money is plain text in mono. No gold, lavender, violet or mint.

Light. A lit line has three layers: a 1 px hot core, a 2 px line in its colour, and a bloom. Glow is static: box-shadow, text-shadow, stacked SVG strokes, or blur baked once into the cached Grid. No CSS filters, SVG blurs, blend modes or backdrop filters, and no shadow, stroke width or blur ever animates. A glow reaches at most 24 px. Chamfered shapes are inline SVG (one polygon stroked four times, split into a line layer and a bloom layer); circles use box-shadow. Animated light is opacity on a pre-glowed layer. A frame is at 30 % idle, 100 % with its bloom when active, 12 % disabled, 25 % when nothing is seated.

Type. Syncopate (700, wide caps) for display: titles, keys, the Result banner, the Refund head. Oxanium (600, squared caps) for labels, names, statuses and panels. JetBrains Mono (700, tabular) for every number. All self-hosted and bundled. Text glow only on display text and lit numbers. Nothing under 14 px at 1280×800. The game screens keep their own faces (Unbounded, Bricolage Grotesque).

Motion. Things rez in as a line of light that opens into the object (380 ms, one flicker at the end), and derez back into a line that goes out (300 ms). Every entrance and exit in the launcher is a rez or a derez, views included. The screen is calm until the story reaches an element. Boot goes one step at a time: the void, the horizon drawn out under the core, the core igniting, the Grid powering on outward, a packet out along every trace, then the ports, clock and gas bar rezzing in turn. Attract's only idle motion is TAP TO START breathing and a lone packet along a side trace every 7 s or so. Never more than three flickers a second, and a flicker never dips below 40 %. Only transform and opacity animate. Reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion) shows end states with short fades: no flicker, no breathing, no packets, no shake.

04Screens

Screen What it shows How you get there and out
Attract / boot The boot sequence, then the Box idling with a breathing TAP TO START. Load. Any tap, click or key leads to Home. Home falls back here after a minute idle.
Home The full machine: ports, block readout and gas bar, core, the focused game's title, facts and Play, the program rack (Quick Draw, Stock Duel, Bet on X sealed, B17 sealed). Focus a program by touching it or with the arrow keys. Play opens Join, or Stake if both seats are full.
Join A seat panel per player: each taps a badge to take a seat. A new badge gets 100 test EGB from the faucet (a transaction). Both seats full → Stake. Back → Home.
Stake Stake choices on light keys (5, 10, 25 EGB), the trust tier, and each seat's TAP TO SIGN. Both taps → the Box opens the match (one transaction, both stakes in). Opened → the game. Back → Home before anyone signs.
Quick Draw The game, under the thin Box overlay. Ends → Result.
Stock Duel The game, under the thin Box overlay. Ends → Result.
Result The winner's port lit, WINNER, the payout transaction running from the core to their port. A draw returns both stakes. Rematch (both seats tap again on Stake) or Home.
Refund Shown when the Box fails to settle in time: the open match, its timeout counted in blocks, then each seat taps to take its stake back (a transaction each), or REFUND ALL sends whatever is still owed (a refund always pays the stake's owner, whoever asks). In Demo mode, the CUT POWER switch in the DEMO tag makes the Box miss its settle during a game. Both refunded → Home.

05Games inside the Box

Inside a game the Box shrinks to a thin overlay: the two ports in the top corners (smaller), the clock at the top centre, and the stake between them. Everything else of the Box steps back; the game has its own design language.

Quick Draw

A western standoff at dusk, drawn in flat silhouette: a long horizon, two seat-coloured figures at the edges, a wooden flip-plate sign in the middle on a post.

  • Wait: the sign reads WAIT…, the light dims, a heartbeat thuds. The draw comes after a random 2–6 s, from a seed both players can't see.
  • Draw: the sign's plate flips to DRAW. First tap wins.
  • Early tap: a tap before the flip loses on the spot (TOO EARLY).
  • The result shows each player's reaction time in milliseconds.

Stock Duel

A painterly live painting: a dark study, a warm lamp on Player 1's side (left), a cyan lamp on Player 2's (right), each pooling light on its player's half of the desk. Brushed-paper textures from layered gradients.

  • The deal. Each player gets four stocks from a 20-stock deck, with numbers recorded from Robinhood Chain's stock pools, frozen at the deal.
  • The hand is a classic brass-and-card Rolodex with index tabs: all four tickers show on the tabs; the front card is face up with the company's name and a draining ring that shows how long it stays in front. Where the stats go there is a ?. No stats on hand cards before the flip. Cards stay upright: the drum indexes one card at a time, turning cards over its top, never tilted on the page.
  • The pick. A tap plays the front card. Once one player has played, the other has one turn of the drum, then their front card is played for them.
  • The stat is chosen last, after both picks, on a brass astrolabe dial: price, average price, 24-hour move or liquidity. It comes from both badges' randomness: (roll1 + roll2) mod 4.
  • The flip. Both cards come out as cream manuscript reveal cards and flip together. The higher number wins; the winner's card gets a green wax seal stamped FINALIZED, the loser's a red one stamped SLASHED. A tie replays the round with the next cards.
  • Best of three. First to two rounds. If the hands run out, the leader wins; level is a draw.

Bet on X and B17 show on Home as sealed programs with a SOON tag and no fake art. They can be focused (their title and facts show) but Play stays dark.

  • apps/box/src/screen/ is Vite + TypeScript with small custom elements and no UI framework.
  • Every game's rules.ts is pure, (state, event) → state, with no clock or randomness of its own (time and seeds arrive in events), and is unit tested.
  • The screen talks only to BoxLink:
interface BoxLink {
  state(): BoxState;                                       // the whole Box, as the screen draws it
  subscribe(fn: (state: BoxState, event: BoxEvent) => void): () => void;
  send(command: ScreenCommand): void;                      // what a touch on the screen asks for
}

BoxState holds the phase, both seats (badge, name, balance), the chain (block, gas), the focused game, the open match with its game state, transactions in flight, and what each tap station's OLED shows. BoxEvent names the moment that changed it (a block, a tap, a transaction step, a buzz, a sound cue), so the screen can animate the moment rather than diff states. ScreenCommand is the touchscreen's vocabulary: focus, play, choose a stake, back, rematch, home, set the opponent.

  • SimLink is this pass's Box: a simulated Box in the browser. It runs the game rules, a fake ledger (EGB balances, a faucet, escrow), a block clock at Sepolia's pace (a block every 12 s, numbered from Sepolia's real height), a gas value that wanders between 0.8 and 4 gwei, the stock deck with recorded prices, and transactions with realistic timing (signed at once, sent ~0.4 s later, in the next block, paid ~1 s after it). Tap stations are simulated behind it: SimLink.tap(seat) is how Demo mode's screen taps and the A / L keys tap, and it is not part of BoxLink. Every simulated transaction has no hash, and the screen shows SIMULATED wherever a hash is missing.
  • LiveLink, a WebSocket to the Box server in src/server/, comes later and implements the same interface. Not built in this pass.

07Demo mode

  • One codebase, one flag: VITE_EGB_MODE=demo|box. demo lets the screen stand in for the tap stations and adds the DEMO tag. box is the bare screen for the Pi's kiosk browser. Until LiveLink exists, both run on SimLink; in box mode both seats are people (no bot), and the screen takes no taps: the real stations (and A / L on a laptop) are the only way to tap. The screen fills the whole window in both.
  • Taps on the screen (Demo mode only): touching a player's frame (their port, or their seat panel on a page) taps that seat. In a game, a touch on a player's half of the screen below the overlay taps for them, with a ring of their light swelling from the touch. A taps Player 1 and L Player 2, in both modes; in Demo mode the ports show their key.
  • Opponent: VS BOT / HOT SEAT in the DEMO tag. With the bot, Player 2 is the bot's badge (its port wears a BOT tag), taps there do nothing, and the bot taps with human timing: a 0.6–2.2 s think in Stock Duel, a 220–420 ms reaction in Quick Draw with a rare early tap.
  • Labelling: the DEMO tag is always visible in the window's bottom-right corner. Opened, it holds CUT POWER (for Refund; the tag reads POWER CUT with a red lamp while it is on), OPPONENT and SOUND, all usable by keyboard. Simulated transaction progress says SIMULATED where a hash would be.
  • A one-time intro panel rezzes in after the boot: two ways to tap (a player's frame or half of the screen, or A / L), and where the opponent switch and the power cut are. OK or Escape dismisses it for good (localStorage gamebox-intro=2, so visitors who saw the old intro see the new taps once).
  • Sound: small synthesized cues with a dark analogue-synth feel for the launcher (boot, tap, block, error, win, lose; the games' seal, flip and tick unchanged), muted from the DEMO tag. localStorage gamebox-muted=1 mutes.

08Constraints

  • 60 fps on a Raspberry Pi 5 browser at 1280×800; scales to 1920×1080 and to a 390×844 phone (a portrait layout of the same objects). Checked so far on a laptop browser (no dropped frames on Home); the Pi run comes with the hardware.
  • The static Grid is drawn once per layout and scale and cached; packets draw on a separate canvas only while one is running. Never more than about 12 layers animate at once.
  • Only transform and opacity animate. Glow is static and reaches at most 24 px: no CSS filters, SVG blurs, blend modes, backdrop filters or animated shadows.
  • Small typed modules with one job each. Every part documented in apps/box/src/screen/README.md.
  • Tests: every game's rules, the simulated ledger, clock and transaction timing, the bot's timing bounds, blockies, and the layout's anchors.
  • pnpm typecheck, pnpm test and pnpm build pass.

09Steps

  1. Scaffold. @egb/box with Vite, Vitest and TypeScript, the scripts, an empty page.
  2. Tokens and parts. tokens.css, self-hosted fonts, the custom elements and their README, the shared types (BoxState, BoxEvent, ScreenCommand, game states, the view contract).
  3. Home and boot. The cached board, layout, stage scaling, router, attract, boot and Home.
  4. Rules with tests. Quick Draw and Stock Duel rules, the deck, their tests.
  5. SimLink and Demo mode. The engine (ledger, clock, gas, transactions), the bot, the pucks, badges, DEMO plate, instruction plate, sound.
  6. Join, Stake, Quick Draw, Result and Refund. The flow end to end.
  7. Stock Duel. Rolodex, astrolabe dial, reveal cards, wax seals.
  8. Deploy. The demo build on Cloudflare Pages, this plan ticked, republished.
  9. The Grid. The launcher restyled as light: tokens and faces, rez and derez, the Grid and light-trail packets, the core, ports, clock and gas bar, chamfered keys, facts, programs, panels and the strip, the pages, the boot, attract and Home in sequence, screen taps and the DEMO tag in place of the desk, the retuned cues.
